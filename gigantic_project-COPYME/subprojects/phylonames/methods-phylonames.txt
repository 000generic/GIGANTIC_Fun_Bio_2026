This is the served methods document for the phylonames subproject. It explains
why the subproject exists, the concepts and vocabulary it introduces, the exact
per-script workflow that produces its outputs, and how those outputs are shared
(both to other GIGANTIC subprojects and to this data server).

This document is also intended as the TEMPLATE for per-subproject methods
documents across GIGANTIC: every subproject gets a methods-<subproject>.txt at
its root, served at the top of its page, following the same section structure
(0 scope, 1 motivation, 2 concepts, 3 inputs, 4 workflow overview, 5 methods by
unit, 6 data products, 7 downstream consumers, 8 reproducibility, 9 limitations,
10 provenance).

0. HOW TO READ THIS DOCUMENT
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

- Sections 1-2 are conceptual: what phylonames are and the vocabulary used
  throughout GIGANTIC. Read these first if the term "phyloname" is new to you.
- Sections 3-6 are operational: the inputs, the step-by-step workflow, the
  individual scripts, and the files produced.
- Sections 7-10 cover how outputs are consumed, how to reproduce them, the
  known limitations, and provenance.

A note on GIGANTIC vocabulary used below (these are precise terms, not
interchangeable):
  - STEP_N      : a sequentially dependent stage of a subproject (STEP_2 needs
                  STEP_1). (A subproject whose stages are independent uses
                  BLOCK_ instead.)
  - workflow-COPYME-*  : the canonical, version-controlled template for a
                  workflow. Users copy it to workflow-RUN_N-* to execute.
  - workflow-RUN_N-*   : one execution instance. Its OUTPUT_pipeline/ holds the
                  real result files.
  - output_to_input/   : a subproject's outputs that OTHER GIGANTIC subprojects
                  consume (inter-subproject hand-off).
  - upload_to_server/  : the curated subset published to this GIGANTIC data
                  server for human browsing.


--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
1. MOTIVATION AND PURPOSE
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Comparative genomics across many species requires ONE consistent, unambiguous,
and phylogenetically informative identifier per species. Common alternatives are
inadequate:

  - Bare "Genus_species" is ambiguous (subspecies/strains collide) and carries
    no information about where a species sits in the tree of life.
  - NCBI taxon IDs are unique but opaque (a number tells a human nothing).
  - Free-text lineages are inconsistent and not machine-friendly.

phylonames solve this. A phyloname encodes a species' full ranked lineage in a
single underscore-delimited token:

    Kingdom_Phylum_Class_Order_Family_Genus_species

so the identifier itself tells you the organism's classification, sorts
sensibly, is safe to use in filenames and table columns, and is stable across
every subproject in a GIGANTIC project.

phylonames is therefore the FIRST subproject run in any GIGANTIC project. Its
output - a mapping from each input species to its phyloname - is the canonical
species identifier consumed by essentially every downstream subproject
(genome database, orthogroups, annotations, species trees, gene trees, OCL
analyses, and so on). See section 7.


--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
2. CONCEPTS AND TERMINOLOGY
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

2.1 GIGANTIC-WIDE CONCEPTS INTRODUCED OR USED HERE

  phyloname (standard format)
      Kingdom_Phylum_Class_Order_Family_Genus_species
      Example: Metazoa_Chordata_Mammalia_Primates_Hominidae_Homo_sapiens
      The most commonly used identifier across GIGANTIC: column headers, table
      values, lookups, and integration between subprojects.

  phyloname_taxonid (extended format)
      phyloname with the NCBI taxon ID appended after a triple underscore:
      Kingdom_Phylum_Class_Order_Family_Genus_species___taxonID
      Example: ...Hominidae_Homo_sapiens___9606
      Used where absolute uniqueness is required (e.g., naming downloaded
      genomic data files, distinguishing strains/subspecies that share a name).

      RULE: "phyloname" and "phyloname_taxonid" are NOT interchangeable. Use the
      term that matches the format actually present.

  Field positions (0-indexed) within a phyloname:
      [0] Kingdom   [1] Phylum   [2] Class   [3] Order
      [4] Family    [5] Genus    [6:] species (join with "_" for multi-word
                                  species/subspecies names)

2.2 CONCEPTS SPECIFIC TO phylonames

  Numbered unknown clades (e.g., Kingdom6555, Family1426)
      NCBI taxonomy is incomplete: many lineages are missing one or more ranked
      levels (no assigned Kingdom, Class, etc.). NCBI taxonomy is also only ONE
      hypothesis of relationships. Rather than leave a gap (which would break the
      fixed 7-field phyloname shape and silently merge unrelated species),
      GIGANTIC fills each missing level with a numbered placeholder of the form
      CladeTypeNumber (no underscore between type and number).

      The number is assigned from the "first named clade below" the missing
      level: all species that share the same first-named-clade-below at a given
      rank receive the SAME number, which groups genuinely related species
      together despite the missing higher taxonomy.

  Clade-splitting artifact (a known limitation - see section 9)
      Because a number is keyed on the first NAMED clade below, a single real
      unknown higher clade that contains several named lower clades gets SPLIT
      into several numbered clades (one per lower clade). This is a workaround,
      not a feature; it can affect analyses whose species span multiple of those
      lower clades. User-provided phylonames (STEP_2) are the fix.

  NOTINNCBI species
      Species absent from NCBI taxonomy entirely (recently described, alternate
      spelling, specialized database). They are NEVER dropped; they receive
      explicit NOTINNCBI placeholders in each missing rank and can be corrected
      with user-provided phylonames in STEP_2.

  User-provided phylonames and the UNOFFICIAL suffix (STEP_2)
      Users may override NCBI-derived phylonames with values from the literature
      or an alternative phylogenetic hypothesis (e.g., to replace numbered
      clades with real names). Any clade level whose value DIFFERS from the
      NCBI-derived value is marked with an UNOFFICIAL suffix (e.g.,
      HolozoaUNOFFICIAL), because assigning a clade is a taxonomic DECISION:
      NCBI made one decision; a user override is, by definition, "unofficial."
      Levels that match NCBI stay unmarked. This keeps the source of every
      assignment transparent. Marking can be disabled globally.


--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
3. INPUTS
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

3.1 Species list (required)
    One species per line, "Genus_species" (underscore, not space). Lines
    beginning with "#" are comments. Resolved in priority order:
      1. workflow-local INPUT_user/species_list.txt (per-run override), else
      2. project-wide INPUT_user/species_set/species_list.txt (default; copied
         into the run's INPUT_user/ at runtime so each run archives a snapshot).

3.2 NCBI taxonomy snapshot (required; obtained by STEP_1 script 001)
    The NCBI "new_taxdump" release. The single critical file within it is
    rankedlineage.dmp, which lists, per taxon, the ranked lineage used to build
    phylonames. How the snapshot is obtained is configurable (see section 8).

3.3 User-provided phylonames (optional; STEP_2 only)
    A TSV of overrides: genus_species, custom_phyloname, unofficial_action
    (ADD_UNOFFICIAL for the standard case; SUPPRESS_UNOFFICIAL to use a value
    as-is). Staged via the project-level INPUT_user/phylonames/ slot, or written
    directly into the STEP_2 workflow's INPUT_user/ for quick runs.


--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
4. WORKFLOW OVERVIEW
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

phylonames is a two-STEP subproject (STEP_2 depends on STEP_1):

    STEP_1  generate_and_evaluate   (always run)
            Obtain the NCBI snapshot -> generate phylonames for ALL NCBI species
            -> extract the project's species into a mapping -> summarize for
            review.

    STEP_2  apply_user_phylonames   (optional)
            Apply user overrides on top of the STEP_1 mapping, with UNOFFICIAL
            marking -> summarize the final mapping.

Each STEP is a NextFlow workflow. The unified driver RUN-workflow.sh runs locally
or self-submits to SLURM based on execution_mode in START_HERE-user_config.yaml.
The shared conda environment (aiG-phylonames) is created automatically on first
run. Python scripts use only the standard library.


--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
5. METHODS BY STEP
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

5.1 STEP_1  -  generate_and_evaluate
    Workflow: STEP_1-generate_and_evaluate/workflow-COPYME-generate_phylonames/
    Scripts live in that workflow's ai/scripts/.

    001_ai-bash-download_ncbi_taxonomy.sh   (obtain the NCBI snapshot)
        Makes an NCBI new_taxdump snapshot available as
        database-ncbi_taxonomy_latest (a directory or symlink containing
        rankedlineage.dmp). Three source modes, set by ncbi_taxonomy.source_mode
        in START_HERE-user_config.yaml:
          download_latest   - download the current new_taxdump from NCBI (default)
          supply_path       - reuse an existing snapshot directory already on
                              disk (no download); the reproducible choice
          download_version  - download a specific dated archive
                              (new_taxdump_YYYY-MM-DD.zip)
        The script validates that rankedlineage.dmp is present and fails fast
        otherwise. See section 8 for why the mode matters.

    002_ai-python-generate_phylonames.py    (generate ALL phylonames)
        Reads rankedlineage.dmp line by line (pipe-delimited: taxon_id,
        taxon_name, species, genus, family, order, class, phylum, kingdom,
        superkingdom). For each taxon it extracts the ranked lineage, cleans
        problematic characters, and fills any missing rank with a numbered
        unknown clade (keyed on the first named clade below; numbers assigned by
        a single counter in file order, so the assignment is deterministic for a
        given snapshot). Outputs (OUTPUT_pipeline/2-output/):
          phylonames                              - every phyloname, one per line
          phylonames_taxonid                      - same, with ___taxonID
          map-phyloname_X_ncbi_taxonomy_info.tsv  - full per-taxon mapping
          map-numbered_clades_X_defining_clades.tsv - numbered clade -> defining
                                                    clade reference
          failed-entries.txt                      - unparseable/empty lines
          generation_metadata.txt                 - counts, timestamp, version
        (2-output is large, ~hundreds of MB per file, and is not published by
        default; most projects only need the small per-project mapping below.)

    003_ai-python-create_species_mapping.py (extract this project's species)
        Joins the input species list against the master mapping from 002 and
        writes the project-specific mapping (OUTPUT_pipeline/3-output/):
          <project>_map-genus_species_X_phylonames.tsv
        Columns: genus_species, phyloname, phyloname_taxonid, source,
        original_ncbi_phyloname  (the same 5-column shape STEP_2 emits, so
        downstream sees one schema regardless of which STEP last ran). For
        STEP_1 every row has source=NCBI and original_ncbi_phyloname=phyloname.

    004_ai-python-generate_taxonomy_summary.py (human review report)
        Produces a readable summary (OUTPUT_pipeline/4-output/, Markdown + HTML):
        taxonomic distribution, which species got numbered clades, and which are
        NOTINNCBI - i.e., exactly the species a user might want to correct in
        STEP_2.

    005_ai-python-write_run_log.py          (lab-notebook record)
        Writes a timestamped run log (species processed, output locations,
        sample mappings, scripts run) into the workflow's ai/logs/.

5.2 STEP_2  -  apply_user_phylonames   (optional)
    Workflow: STEP_2-apply_user_phylonames/workflow-COPYME-apply_user_phylonames/

    001_ai-python-apply_user_phylonames.py  (apply overrides + UNOFFICIAL)
        Takes the STEP_1 mapping and the user-provided phylonames TSV. For each
        overridden species, replaces the phyloname and marks each clade level
        that differs from the NCBI-derived value with UNOFFICIAL (unless
        suppressed). Outputs (OUTPUT_pipeline/1-output/):
          final_project_mapping.tsv      - the final 5-column mapping (this is
                                           the authoritative project mapping)
          unofficial_clades_report.tsv   - which clades were marked UNOFFICIAL
        Rows touched by the user get source=USER and retain
        original_ncbi_phyloname.

    002_ai-python-generate_taxonomy_summary.py (review report, post-override)
        Same summary as STEP_1 004, reflecting the user overrides
        (OUTPUT_pipeline/2-output/, Markdown + HTML).

    003_ai-python-write_run_log.py          (lab-notebook record)


--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
6. DATA PRODUCTS
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

6.1 output_to_input/   (consumed by OTHER GIGANTIC subprojects)

    The canonical hand-off is the per-project mapping, exposed three ways:

      output_to_input/STEP_1-generate_and_evaluate/maps/
          <project>_map-genus_species_X_phylonames.tsv   (NCBI-only mapping)
      output_to_input/STEP_2-apply_user_phylonames/maps/
          <project>_map-genus_species_X_phylonames.tsv   (with user overrides)
      output_to_input/maps/
          <project>_map-genus_species_X_phylonames.tsv   (CONVENIENCE symlink;
          points to whichever STEP ran most recently - STEP_2 if it was run,
          else STEP_1)

    All entries are symlinks into the canonical workflow-RUN_N OUTPUT_pipeline,
    so there is one set of real files and a single source of truth. Downstream
    subprojects read output_to_input/maps/ and therefore automatically pick up
    user overrides when STEP_2 has been run.

6.2 upload_to_server/   (curated, published to this data server)

    Publishing is manifest-driven (it is NOT a direct copy from the workflow).
    Each workflow's upload_manifest.tsv lists which OUTPUT_pipeline files to
    publish and how to label them. The subproject-level helper
    RUN-update_upload_to_server.sh reads those manifests and assembles
    upload_to_server/ as symlinks; the data server then follows those symlinks.
    For phylonames the published set is the per-project species mapping (the
    species list you see on this server) and the human-readable taxonomy
    summaries. The very large master files from STEP_1 2-output are not
    published by default.

7. DOWNSTREAM CONSUMERS
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

phylonames must run first; essentially every per-species subproject reads
output_to_input/maps/, including:
  - genomesDB            (names standardized proteome files by phyloname)
  - orthogroups          (labels orthogroup membership by phyloname)
  - annotations_hmms     (keys per-species annotation files by phyloname)
  - trees_species        (labels species-tree leaves by phyloname)
  - trees_gene_families / trees_gene_groups (label gene-tree tips by phyloname)
  - ocl_phylogenetic_structures and the integrator (propagate phyloname labels
    through evolutionary OCL analyses and combined tables)
  - gene_sizes, homolog_counts, hotspots, secretome, one_direction_homologs, ...


--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
8. REPRODUCIBILITY
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Two facts govern reproducibility of phylonames:

  (a) The generation is DETERMINISTIC. Given a fixed rankedlineage.dmp, script
      002 always produces the identical master list (numbered clades included),
      because numbers are assigned by a single counter in file order with no
      randomness.

  (b) Numbered clades DEPEND ON THE EXACT NCBI SNAPSHOT. The number assigned to
      a given clade depends on the counter state over the WHOLE file, so a
      DIFFERENT NCBI snapshot (a different download date) can change numbered
      clades, and NCBI itself revises named lineages over time. Therefore named
      lineages are stable, but numbered clades are only stable across runs that
      use the SAME snapshot.

To make a project's phylonames reproducible, PIN the snapshot:
  - source_mode: supply_path  with taxonomy_path pointing at a preserved
    snapshot directory (the recommended approach once a project has a snapshot),
    or
  - source_mode: download_version  with a dated NCBI archive.
A project that pins its snapshot can regenerate byte-identical phylonames at any
time. download_latest is convenient for a first run but is not reproducible,
because "latest" moves.


--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
9. LIMITATIONS AND KNOWN ARTIFACTS
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

  - Clade-splitting (section 2.2): a single real unknown higher clade containing
    several named lower clades is split into several numbered clades. Use STEP_2
    user-provided phylonames to correct cases that matter for an analysis.

  - Numbered clades are snapshot-dependent (section 8). They are GIGANTIC
    placeholders, not NCBI assignments, and should be treated as such. Named
    clades are the stable, citable part of a phyloname.

  - NOTINNCBI species carry placeholder phylonames until corrected in STEP_2.

  - NCBI taxonomy is incomplete and represents one hypothesis of relationships;
    a phyloname reflects that classification, not an independent phylogenetic
    inference.


--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
10. PROVENANCE AND VERSIONING
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

  - The mapping served here is this project's fixed, authoritative species
    mapping (the final mapping after any user overrides). Its named lineages
    follow the NCBI new_taxdump snapshot in use when the mapping was generated;
    its numbered clades are GIGANTIC placeholders specific to that snapshot
    (section 8).
  - Each downloaded snapshot records its own download_metadata.txt (source URL,
    date, checksum). A pinned snapshot kept under the project's
    INPUT_user/ncbi_taxonomy/ carries that provenance forward and allows exact
    regeneration.
  - Every workflow run writes a timestamped run log to its ai/logs/ directory,
    and AI-assisted development sessions are captured to the project research
    notebook, so the full chain of how these outputs were produced is recorded.
